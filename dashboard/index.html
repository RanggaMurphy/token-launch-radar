How a row is priced

  1. Group ratio. Protocols are grouped by what they do. Each group's tokenised launches give a market-cap-to-TVL ratio; the board uses the group's median, shrunk toward the market-wide median and capped at the richest ratio the snapshot records at that size.
  2. Growth. The seven-day TVL change scales the value — a protocol gathering TVL into a launch is worth more than one leaking it. The change is clamped, so one wild week cannot carry a rank.
  3. Vintage. Time tracked with no token recorded scales the value from 0.8× to 1.2×: a launch is worth more when the protocol has already run for years without one.
  4. The band. Every value is drawn with the same formula run at its group's 25th and 75th percentile ratios — the spread of what comparable launches actually captured, shown beside each value and shaded on the chart.

The full method, with every constant, is on the Method tab; open any row for its own workings.

What the board is

The snapshot is DefiLlama's protocol list as of the date in the header: every protocol with at least the floor of TVL the file states, each with ten fields — name, category, chains, TVL, seven-day TVL change, market cap, the date tracking began, and the CoinGecko id and symbol of its token, either of which is null when the record names none.

Who gets ranked

A protocol is ranked when it records no token — both its CoinGecko id and its symbol are null — and it is not a wrapped, staked or bridged product of a protocol whose token already trades. The product test uses every part of the rule the snapshot's fields can check: the category is not Bridge, Canonical Bridge, Liquid Staking, Liquid Restaking, Restaked BTC or CEX; the name ends in no version marker; the name carries no whole word wrapped, staked or staking; and the name does not begin with an exchange's name. The field guide's own test also reads a parent-protocol field the snapshot does not carry, so a sub-product identifiable only by that link cannot be screened from this file; the Method says so rather than pretend otherwise. The snapshot's watchlist is covered in full — it is the floor of the board, never its ceiling.

What a launch would be worth

Implied value = TVL × group ratio × growth × vintage.

Group ratio. Protocols are grouped by what they do (the table below). For each group, take every tokenised protocol in the snapshot with a market cap, compute its market-cap-to-TVL ratio, clip it to [0.01, 3], and take the median — then shrink it toward the market-wide median by the group's sample size, so a group priced off five launches borrows the market's evidence: ratio = (n × group median + 8 × global median) / (n + 8). Finally the ratio is capped at the richest the snapshot records at that size: no tokenised protocol with $250M or more of TVL trades above 0.45× TVL, and none with $50M or more trades above 1.05×.

Growth. 1 + clamp(seven-day TVL change, −25%, +25%) × 1.5 / 100. An unreported change counts as 0. Vintage. 0.8 + 0.4 × min(days tracked, 1460) / 1460; a protocol with no listing date takes the neutral 0.95. A launch is worth more when the protocol has already run for years with no token recorded.

The band beside each value runs the same formula at the group's shrunk 25th and 75th percentile ratios. It is the spread of what comparable launches actually captured — some launches price at a tenth of their TVL, a few at several times it — not a confidence interval.

Conviction tiers. The board also grades each protocol's record, not its value: Proven scale — at least $100M of TVL and at least a year tracked with no token; Building — at least $25M and at least six months; Early — everything else, including any protocol whose age the record omits. The tier filters and sorts nothing by itself; it is the at-a-glance answer to "how much record stands behind this rank?"

What the board does not claim

It says a protocol records no token; a token DefiLlama does not track would not appear. It never treats a value as a price: every figure is TVL, a ratio read off the snapshot's own launches, or the product of the two, and each row's detail view shows its workings, its comparable launches and its risks. Farm plans are generated from each protocol's own record — its product, its first chain's fees, its TVL's capital tier and its age's hold window — and each step states its cost and its time.
